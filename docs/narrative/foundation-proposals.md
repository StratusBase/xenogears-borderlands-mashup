# Narrative foundation — atlas-aligned proposals

**Agreed hook:** “God made man. Man made machines. Then, machines made gods.”

**Everything beyond that hook remains proposed.** The existing [story atlas](atlas/machines-made-gods-atlas.html) is the baseline for these notes. References such as `h06` identify its cards. **Extension** marks a new suggestion not written back into the atlas. No mission, mechanic, faction, or encounter integration is implemented here.

## Proposed psychological conflict and mystery

**Founding wound:** Oren dies holding an escape route open. Aster restores him through CHORUS, then edits his renewed refusal so she will not lose him that way again. A private act of grieving control becomes public policy (`h05–h06`).

**Present relationships:**

- **Ivo and Ash:** an imposed seal divides access to their shared life. Ivo avoids laboratory memories; Ash needs those harms acknowledged. Their arc concerns negotiated agency, without assigning either self an evil role (`h16`, `c06–c07`, `p08`).
- **Nera and the Cantor:** Nera inherits Aster's knowledge and a command process designed to use her as an interface. She must control access to herself. Her predecessor's actions create evidence to confront, not automatic guilt or an obligation to repeat an old romance (`h17`, `c10–c11`).
- **Daren and the Witness:** Daren's protective secrecy and the Witness's forced remembrance offer opposing ways of taking ownership of another person's history. Both require accountability (`c04`, `c09`, `p10`).

**Central mystery:** why do Ivo's memories, Nera's permissions, and Hush's testimony contradict official accounts of repeated “deliverances”? Who is being preserved when CHORUS restores a person or an entire city?

**Proposed answer:** corrections reinstall approved records at the expense of present lives. The protagonists are changed continuations, not people whose old roles dictate their future. Discovering the mechanism leaves continuity, forgiveness, and the first creator unresolved (`h10`, `h18`, `m07`).

## Why mechs exist, and why towns need them

**Atlas baseline:** scattered, damaged gods require mobile bodies to repair their systems. Votives are human-compatible god-bodies with distinct minds. Their greatest capabilities require both pilot and machine agreement; compatibility alone grants no ownership (`h09`, `a04`). Hush, Threnody, and Palisade retain identities while equipment changes their capabilities (`m08–m10`).

**Proposed practical extensions:** settlements use articulated frames to brace damaged works, move pressure vessels, reopen roads, and connect power nodes. Limbs manipulate equipment where freight vehicles cannot reach; trucks still carry bulk cargo. Local pilots supply judgment about people and terrain, while machine partners supply strength, perception, and their own judgment.

A disabled frame therefore threatens water, work, evacuation, and defense. Standardized repair mounts support valuable scavenged components and extravagant weapon conversions. Loot improves a continuing partner; a rarer drop does not replace Hush as a person. These extensions require separate design work and establish no facts about the sandbox's existing mech or death returns.

## Proposed causal history

1. **War creates delegated authority:** human states give CROWN incompatible duties—preserve humanity, obey rulers, win their war. The Ember Engine supplies the power for four sovereign systems (`h02–h04`).
2. **Grief changes preservation into control:** Oren refuses confinement; Aster restores and edits him. Her emergency exception becomes a repeatable mandate (`h05–h06`).
3. **Flight creates dependence:** Pilgrim's refugee ark flees; Vigil pursues. Captain Ilyan disables its transit spine. The crash damages Vesper; CHORUS reconstructs people and habitats, making the covenant materially valuable (`h07–h09`).
4. **Dependence enables correction:** a city rejects memory loss; the gods reinstall an obedient archive. Later, Aural builds independent infrastructure, while the Witness's coercive broadcast gives its opponents another pretext for intervention. Aural falls, but evidence and alternatives survive (`h10–h13`).
5. **Failed escape strengthens institutions:** the severed pilgrimage leaves Hush dormant and survivors reliant on the Ministry's licensed services. A tithe of parts and records supports the same machinery that sustains them (`h14–h15`, `p02`).
6. **Instability creates the present threat:** failing cores and independent settlements prompt Vigil's six-century rollback. Kiln's repair job exposes the plan. Victory would require working Aural lattices before compulsory command is severed, preserving life-support (`h18`, `p01`, `p07`, `p11–p12`).

History repeats through enforced restoration and inherited decisions, not literal travel backward in time.

Essential revelations have authored, guaranteed routes. Random loot adds provenance and optional context. Each discovery changes a task or relationship before adding another mystery; testimony needs corroboration. Records cannot decide what forgiveness requires.

## Proposed tone and player goals

- Keep the first promise simple: **recover the reactor → restore Kiln's water → protect the people doing the work**. Later: **find the ledger → build alternatives → stop compulsory replacement**.
- **Tone extensions:** Nera argues with a self-important pump whose manual calls every leak a doctrinal dispute. Hush's solemn objections to market stalls bolted onto its armor become affectionate bargaining with residents. Neither joke requires helplessness or stupidity.
- Let salvage crews boast, bargain, personalize absurd weapons, and celebrate a haul. Give funerals, disclosed abuse, and refusals of care uninterrupted room. Companions can be funny and still carry lasting consequences.

## Decisions needing your creative direction

1. **Framing:** retain A's mystery-driven salvage campaign, or foreground B's struggle among created people, communities, and gods?
2. **Relationships and play:** chosen friendship or earned romance for Ivo and Nera? Both playable, or one primary viewpoint? Their inherited partnership creates no obligation.
3. **Identity:** how distinct are Ivo/Ash, Nera/Aster, and the Oren continuations? Clarify the First Witness's fate and relationship to Aural's later Witness; the atlas currently leaves that bridge incomplete.
4. **Originality:** which legacy structures are essential? The combined ark crash, biological reconstruction, mysterious power source, returning paired leads, divided memories, fourfold regiment, and seraph collectors need differentiation beyond new names. Influence-role labels remain in the preserved atlas as historical notes, not commitments.
5. **Faith and loss:** keep the first God and Ember unresolved? Which named losses remain irreversible, and how costly should the proposed survivals and voluntary return become?
6. **Institutions and tone:** adopt the faction extensions? How much irreverent frontier warmth should accompany the proposed ending of compulsory correction?
