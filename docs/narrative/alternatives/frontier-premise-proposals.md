# Narrative foundation — PROPOSALS

**Status: creative proposals; nothing here is adopted canon.** Every fictional detail and future design below is proposed. A and B are alternatives; shared proposals are candidates for either.

**Scope:** documentation only. The current encounter remains independent of these proposals. Existing title, brands, and mechanics establish no lore here; proposed missions and discoveries are unimplemented.

## Proposed creative approach

- **Frontier energy:** dangerous salvage, improvised towns, rival crews, extravagant weapons, bargaining, and expressive companions. A good haul buys breathing room for someone specific.
- **Xenogears, not Xenosaga, is the thematic reference:** identity, repression, responsibility, religious meaning, and institutions formed by old survival decisions. Propose original cosmology, relationships, and history rather than counterparts to its characters or artifacts.
- **Human center:** a pilot who treats usefulness as the price of being loved. Relationships challenge that belief; discoveries do more than explain machinery.

## Proposal A — The Debt of the Dead

**Premise:** On a drought frontier, families pledge rights to recorded working knowledge for water and repairs. A salvage pilot buys a frame containing their dead mentor's reflexes—and fragments of a disaster the mentor supposedly prevented. Salvage becomes a struggle over who owns the dead's testimony. Creditors control certified copies and replacement-part calibration keys; they cannot repossess skills people have already learned.

- **Immediate goal:** recover a seized pump and escort its repair crew home before the reserve runs dry. Water is restored without first solving the historical mystery.
- **Psychological conflict:** the pilot's celebrated rescue maneuver is inherited training; its use also brings grief they cannot distinguish from their own. They want to deserve their reputation without surrendering their last connection to the mentor. A former crewmate wants acknowledgment of the disaster, not another heroic memorial.
- **Central mystery:** why do three frames attribute both the evacuation cutoff order and objections to the mentor, while the official record claims unanimous approval? Can a recorded sensation establish consent or guilt?
- **Possible authorial answer:** emergency software pooled workers' sensory traces under the mentor's operational certificate. A tribunal knowingly recast permission to operate as the crews' collective approval of abandoning an outer district, protecting an insolvent relief fund from compensation claims. The mentor chose a devastating triage; the ruling erased objections and distributed blame.
- **Larger question:** does inheriting an ability create obligations toward its source's unfinished wrongs? Preserving a trace may honor someone or perpetuate exploitation. Whether a trace continues a person remains open.

**Proposed emphasis:** intimate grief, contested memory, labor ownership, and the difference between forgiveness and acquittal.

## Proposal B — A Country That Hasn't Happened

**Premise:** Frontier towns invest in routes to regions forecast to become habitable. A convoy pilot breaks an evacuation plan to rescue their brother, detouring a maintenance frame to stabilize his failing well. The supposedly safe destination then loses its scheduled repair and collapses. Authorities claim the pilot either disproved prophecy or caused the catastrophe.

- **Immediate goal:** escort the stranded water convoy to a working well and negotiate shelter. Success means water and somewhere to sleep.
- **Psychological conflict:** the pilot confuses being indispensable with being loved. Their brother resents becoming proof of exceptional destiny. Sharing responsibility means accepting choices the pilot cannot guarantee will be safe.
- **Central mystery:** do forecasts describe possible futures, or do institutions manufacture them? Why do some forecasts predict failures at healthy wells months before their maintenance is withdrawn?
- **Possible authorial answer:** models combine environmental measurements with assumed policy and human compliance. Authorities route resources using those forecasts, making favored routes safer and alternatives more dangerous. Hidden allocation assumptions masquerade as natural inevitability; sincere obedience reinforces the cycle.
- **Larger question:** what do we owe people who may exist tomorrow, and who may consent for them? The resource feedback loop becomes demonstrable; a few unexplained warnings can remain spiritually ambiguous.

**Proposed emphasis:** anticipatory grief, providence, freedom, and responsibility under uncertainty. The protagonist remains a fallible worker.

## Shared proposal — three factions

These institutions provide necessary services. Their disputes should survive the discovery of technical facts; sincere faith and ethical disagreement remain meaningful.

**Proposed religious practice:** the Open Hand teaches that a person's sacred worth exceeds their usefulness. Communal meals leave a place for those who cannot answer: the dead in A, the unborn in B. Whether custodians may speak for that empty place divides believers; mutual care retains value whatever the machines reveal.

| Proposed faction | Belief and material incentive | Contradiction and internal divide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Open Hand** | “No life is ours alone.” Runs clinics, funeral houses, and convoy shelters. Needs donations, trained carers, and access to frames. In A, safeguards the dead's obligations; in B, advocates for future strangers. | Offers unconditional care but makes access to sensitive records depend on religious custodianship. Some elders protect the vulnerable through secrecy; other believers demand informed consent even if disclosure costs the order its authority. |
| **The Measure Office** | “Fairness requires a measure everyone can inspect.” Certifies water shares, safety standards, and contracts. Needs fees and predictable production. In A, registers skill ownership; in B, certifies routes. | Calls its accounting neutral while excluding losses it cannot price. Field auditors quietly break allocation rules to save towns; headquarters fears that exceptions will collapse the common system. |
| **The Loose Company** | “No one owns another person's work or tomorrow.” A federation of salvage crews and independent settlements. Needs open salvage rights, affordable parts, and bargaining strength. | Defends freedom while captains monopolize scarce equipment and shift maintenance costs onto outsiders. Cooperative crews want shared dues; prosperous captains call those dues another tax. |

**Proposed conflict engine:** the Office can finance repairs but demand control; the Hand can shelter people but restrict disclosure; the Company can recover machinery but demand salvage rights. No faction alone can keep the frontier alive. Ordinary members have reasons to cooperate across faction lines.

## Shared proposal — why mechs matter

- **Civilian origin:** frames brace failing shafts, lift pressure vessels, and replace parts in unstable terrain. Limbs manipulate fittings beyond the reach of wheeled heavy vehicles; trucks carry ordinary freight.
- **Why pilots:** interference disrupts remote control near active wells. Local operators improvise through force feedback; automation handles balance and routine safety.
- **Dependence:** frames maintain water and cooling equipment, clear routes, and protect crews. A wreck threatens wages, survival, and defense. Repair schools, pilot associations, and parts creditors gain power.
- **Why loot and weapons:** standardized mounts make salvage reusable. Cutters, anchors, and charge launchers acquire combat variants; dedicated weapons arm crews competing over routes and supplies.
- **Option distinction:** A's sensory calibration teaches inherited skills while leaking emotional fragments; B's frames gather surveys and maintain forecast routes.

## Proposed causal histories

Dates remain undecided; the arrows express causes, not just an order of events.

**Shared trunk:** intensive extraction lowers aquifers → towns require deeper, dangerous wells → versatile maintenance frames become essential → pooled repair funds and safety certification prevent avoidable deaths → institutions controlling certification and parts gain authority over settlement survival.

**A branch:** a lethal training shortage prompts skill recording → families pledge reproduction rights for loans → creditors enforce exclusive licenses while religious custodians defend the dead's claims → a flood evacuation pools traces → the tribunal knowingly converts operational authority into collective approval → compensation is denied, leaving districts unable to repay repair loans → repossession and resale scatter contradictory evidence → unauthorized copying becomes a survival trade → the pilot's purchased frame threatens the ruling behind the pump seizure.

**B branch:** dwindling wells force migration → survey models reduce convoy deaths → towns borrow against forecast water access → certification directs maintenance, credit, and protection toward approved routes → alternatives become less safe and less observable → apparent confirmation turns advice into civic duty and religious expectation → independent towns fund illicit scouts → the pilot's detour exposes the forecast's dependence on compliance → stranded borrowers and rival authorities fight over the next route designation.

**Proposed historical pattern:** emergency powers become ordinary institutions. Later generations repeat the founding sacrifice because livelihoods depend on its rules.

## Proposed discovery through play

| Channel | A example | B example | What the player can do with it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Exploration** | Find an abandoned training annex outside the officially recorded evacuation boundary. | Scout a rejected route with intact wells missing from the approved map. | Open a practical alternative and question an authoritative account. |
| **Equipment** | Compare a salvaged actuator's certificate with an independently preserved repair ledger. | Match survey module revisions to dates when towns lost coverage. | Establish provenance; decide who gets access to a useful part and its evidence. |
| **Environment** | Rescue winches face toward the district described as already empty. | Maintenance trenches stop exactly at a forecast boundary, although the fault continues. | Form a hypothesis from a place before anyone explains it. |
| **Character choice** | Share a painful trace with its source's family, whose wishes may conflict with public disclosure. | Ask the convoy to accept a risky alternate route instead of issuing another unilateral rescue order. | Change trust, access, and who bears a known cost; nobody must become a villain to disagree. |

**Proposed discovery rule:** essential evidence has guaranteed locations or conversations; random equipment adds corroboration. Follow **find → compare independent evidence → make a consequential choice**. Records establish events without settling forgiveness or justice. Show likely costs before a choice; no hidden morality score.

## Shared proposal — tone and immediate goals

- Keep visible goals: **recover the pump → get the crew home → restore water** in A; **reach the well → secure shelter → repair the convoy** in B. Each job changes daily life before opening another question.
- **Nell “Warranty” Voss**, a potential mechanic companion, argues that every catastrophic breakdown is “unauthorized enthusiasm.” She also refuses a profitable salvage job until a trapped rival is recovered.
- **Brother Pell**, a potential Open Hand medic, conducts ridiculous auctions of donated luxuries to fund care. His private mourning ritual stays quiet, without a joke interrupting its consequence.
- Let crews boast, bicker, customize machinery, and celebrate loot. Aim jokes at vanity, profiteering, bureaucracy, and affectionate friction. Give injury, belief, and grief room to matter.
