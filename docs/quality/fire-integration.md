# Full hybrid Fire integration

`src/rendering/quality/gameElements.ts` is the normal Enhanced registration map. It composes `fireModule` beside Radiation and retains the existing corrosion fallback. `GamePresentation` defaults to that map and passes the real weapon root with its shot notification, so only the firing rig receives the cartridge heat pulse. Legacy remains reversible. The shared root also exposes `createGameElementsBeforeFire` for a same-build baseline with every other registered module preserved.

Fire owns its artwork, atlas lease, material/particle response, equipment and flight modules, cosmetic event variation and sound recipes. It consumes existing contact, detonation, secondary-recipient and status notifications. It does not apply damage, change status/fuse timing, move actors, alter collision, shift muzzles, mutate frozen weapon recipes or consume gameplay randomness. The optional generic flight omissions prevent duplicated channels/casings in composition.

`FireResponse.prepare(renderer, scene)` uploads the shared atlas, prepares linear offscreen variants, and warms the actual HalfFloat overlay pipeline using private templates. The offscreen draw never fires or steps the Game and never attaches a dummy overlay to a combat target. The existing first render schedules preparation in normal gameplay; review capture explicitly awaits it. All retained templates are disposed with the response. Transient projectile materials hold their own atlas leases, so switching presentation does not invalidate active shots.

Lingering roots project back onto their already-resolved visible source, avoiding repeated whole-body searches without leaking onto an underlying material or equipment slot. Primary impact resolution still selects the visible outer shell. Fire imports visible-surface and contact projection from neutral `quality/shared/` helpers. Borrowed geometry/materials/raycast functions remain intact. The explicit `projectVisibleSurface` helper reuses the established triangle projection for floor/wall/sloped/underside supports without reparenting or retagging them. Flame pools, impact cards, scorch bindings and contacts have finite caps; pause and clear preserve the established lifecycle.

Combat-lead handoff: no Fire edits to `src/game.ts`, `src/world.ts` or `src/ui.ts` are required. They retain collision and combat ownership. Concurrent Radiation integration has its own authorized `game.ts` changes; those are not part of the Fire patch. Shared registration coordination is recorded in `production-elements-coordination.md`.

Review entry: build with `npx vite build --config vite.fire-full.config.ts`, preview with that config, and open `/fire-full-lab.html`. It uses the production factory, actual Game/ballistics/statuses, an isolated review save and optional intact clothed/armored human fixtures. Its before/current/repeated-before driver holds scene, weapons, input, seed, fixed simulation step and sound constant. Visual timing, native recordings, source hashes and measured receipts are kept separately from design claims.

Direction verification: the review entry has `Fire next shot / keep scene`, which advances the existing Game and respects its real cooldown/ammunition without replacing the renderer or reseeding the effect. Six captured continuing shots show different left/right/up bearings, six real trigger records, and 30→24 ammunition. A separate native recording can hold fire on the same target. The effect chooses a stable six-sector direction once per actual event; only its curves evolve during the lifetime.

Required equipment alignment review is complete with Claude Opus 5.5 against A/B/C boards, pilot/mech idle/fire/reload frames, mixed channels, timed detonation and surface views plus source. The peer confirms palette/direction/support improvements but retains a thin-glyph C gap and small vent/soot readability limits. Mech vent origins are not conclusively exposed by these views. Full findings, evidence limits, native sound captures and matched measurements are recorded in [fire-verification.md](fire-verification.md). Peer review does not certify native motion or sound and is not user approval. The user explicitly authorized full normal-game integration in this task.

Captured-shot lifetime: `ElementModule.flight` supplies resource-independent Fire decorators and animation for already-fired projectiles after a Legacy toggle. This retains Fire art on subsequent classic Cluster children and keeps their atlas leased until ballistic disposal. World responses stay disposed and never respawn. An automated regression verifies live child art/animation, real carrier deployment, final atlas release and absence of revived world effects.

The asset builder adds presentation-only `equipmentMuzzleIndex` metadata to actual sockets; Fire uses it for source-local arm gating without changing socket transforms or projectile origins. Unmapped chambers remain diagnostic and do not default to arm0. Pilot idle seams cool while the shot release reads more clearly.
